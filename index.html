Question and expectations

What I ask, and what I expect

How has enforcement of the headscarf (hijab) restriction in Kazakh schools changed since the Supreme Court ruling of September 2024, through 2026?

The rule: no religious clothing in public and private schools. The ruling: a school may not refuse a student entry. The rule itself stayed.

Expectation 1

Enforcement continued through tools other than refusing entry, such as fines on parents.

Expectation 2

After the ruling, some new tools appear in the sources only as public statements, and few or none as confirmed actions.

Empirical, not normative: I describe how enforcement changed, not whether it should have.

Theory

Three ideas behind the toolbox

So I expect other tools, applied school by school, under a secular reason.

LevelIdeaSources
StateA government can obey a court on one point and keep control by other, legal-looking means.Ginsburg & Moustafa (2008); Varol (2015)
SchoolA national rule is applied school by school. Heads decide, talks often come first, and officials may deny a ban exists.Atwill (2004); Montpetit (2026, working paper); Mamaraimov & Asanova (IWPR); Forum 18 (2018)
ContextSecularism is the stated reason, an old reflex now mixed with security.Bashirov (2020); Boron (2024); Sikhimbayeva et al. (2025)

Mostly European sources: ideas to test in Kazakhstan, not proof.

Concepts

Three concepts, defined and checked

Ladder of abstraction: concept, then tool, then indicator. Enforcement, then refusing entry, then a documented refusal.

ConceptMy definitionQuality
EnforcementAny action, formal or informal, by the state or by school staff, meant to make students follow the dress rule.Broad on purpose; the list of tools bounds it.
Headscarf restrictionThe dress rule itself: it bars religious clothing in public and private schools. It says what is required, not what is done.Clear and checkable; says nothing about practice.
ChangeA difference in which tools are in use before the ruling (to Sept 2024) and after it (to 2026).Easy to observe, but crude: no frequency.

Design and case

A descriptive study of one case

ElementWhat I do
DesignDescriptive: I document what happened with checkable records. Not positivist (no general law), not interpretivist (no meanings).
CaseKazakhstan’s schools after the 2024 ruling: a case of what enforcement looks like after a court closes one tool but leaves the rule in place.
Why this caseA prominent case: a recent, visible ruling that limits one tool, so before and after can be compared.
ScopeAll schools, public and private; before the ruling (to Sept 2024) and after it (to 2026).
MethodDocument analysis: I sort each item by how confirmed it is, then compare tools before and after.
